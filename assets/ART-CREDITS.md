# About the cover

*An imaginary arrival at Saint-Lazare* is a contemporary digital collage. The train station comes from Claude Monet. The small airship above the platform comes from a separate woodcut of Henri Giffard's steam-powered dirigible. Their encounter is imagined.

**The station.** Claude Monet, *Arrival of the Normandy Train, Gare Saint-Lazare*, 1877. Oil on canvas, 60.3 × 80.2 cm. Art Institute of Chicago, Mr. and Mrs. Martin A. Ryerson Collection, accession 1933.1158. The banner uses a horizontal detail of the public-domain painting. The digital reproduction used here is from Google Arts & Culture via Wikimedia Commons. [Museum collection record](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/16571/arrival-of-the-normandy-train-gare-saint-lazare) · [Museum IIIF record](https://api.artic.edu/api/v1/artworks/16571/manifest.json) · [Reproduction and public-domain notice](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Claude_Monet_-_Arrival_of_the_Normandy_Train,_Gare_Saint-Lazare_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

**The airship.** Perot, woodcut of Henri Giffard's 1852 steam-powered airship. NASA on The Commons identifies the engraver as Perot and the image as NASM-00102058. The collection lists the reproduction under “No known copyright restrictions.” The banner isolates the envelope, rigging and gondola, reduces their scale and tints the ink to sit within the painted steam.
